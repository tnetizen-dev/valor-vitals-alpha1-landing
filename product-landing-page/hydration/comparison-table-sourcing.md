## 🛑 The chloride row will backfire — recommend cutting it

Valor declares **1,200mg chloride**. LMNT does not declare chloride at all, so the cell would read as a dash.

But 800mg of sodium delivered as sodium chloride carries roughly **1,230mg of chloride** — which is almost exactly Valor's declared figure. Our chloride isn't an added ingredient; it's the chloride that comes with our salt.

Run the same arithmetic on LMNT: 1,000mg sodium as sodium chloride implies roughly **1,540mg chloride** — *more than ours*. They simply don't declare it.

A reader who does that arithmetic — and this is the most label-literate audience in the category, by Ian's own description — will conclude the table is being cute. **Cut the chloride row from the comparison table.**

Chloride can stay in the ingredient list in `S4_FORMULA`, but the framing has to be accurate: the honest claim is *we declare it and most brands don't*, not *we have more of it*. Worth a look at that section's wording before launch.

## Still missing

1. **LMNT calories and total carbohydrate.** Their own panel images weren't machine-readable. Likely 5–10 calories, 1–2g carb, but unconfirmed.
2. **Nuun Sport vitamin C.** One source says not listed; Nuun has carried vitamin C in some SKUs historically. Needs a current label.
3. **Liquid I.V. magnesium and chloride.** Both appear absent from the original Hydration Multiplier formula, but "appears absent from a third-party summary" is not the same as confirmed.

## Why I would not print what's above

- Every figure came from third-party summaries or brand marketing pages, not from a current label. Aggregator sites lag reformulations, sometimes by years.
- Liquid I.V. has reformulated and expanded its line; the figures may belong to a different SKU than the one a reader would pick up.
- Serving sizes differ — Nuun is a tablet in ~16oz, LMNT a stick in 16–32oz, Liquid I.V. a stick in 16oz. Per-serving comparison is roughly fair but not identical, and a footnote should say so.
- The section opens with *"You don't have to take our word for it. Look at the numbers."* That sentence raises the standard of proof on everything under it.

## Recommended sourcing method

Buy one of each, photograph the supplement facts panel, date the photograph, build the table from those images, and keep the photos on file. Roughly $60–100 and an afternoon. It is the only method that survives a competitor complaint or a customer who checks.

Add a dated footnote to the table: *"Figures taken from published labels, [month year]. Formulations change — check the panel on whatever you're using."* That line also reinforces the argument the section is making.
